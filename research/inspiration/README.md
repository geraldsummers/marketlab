## What we tested and what happened

A bounded literature and documentation orientation covered Buffett/Pritzker,
Thorp, Alluvial, AQR and Man research; an issuer tender amendment; LDI forced
selling; USDC banking disruption; WTI delivery constraints; prediction resolution;
and auction keepers. We inspected public primary publications, not experiment
outcome datasets, prices, fills or independently audited manager performance.
This was roadmap orientation, not a historical strategy experiment.

## What we know

The sources identify economic details worth making explicit: contractual rights,
capital lockup, financing, access, forced sellers, operational costs and failed
theses. Each [case record](cases/) separates the source's statement from our
inference and names its evidence limitations. Primary does not mean independent.
Manager returns and public holdings are not complete strategy records.

## What we suspect

Document-intensive events may suit this lab better than another generic short-term
return model. This is a research-priority judgment, not evidence of profitability.
Small practitioners and market operators receive the same attention as famous
managers; obscurity alone does not imply an edge. Success stories are selected
retrospectively, and failure cases and ordinary periods must enter any sample.

## Roadmap and next permitted action

All three audits are P1 with selection ranks 10, 20 and 30. Existing priorities
still take precedence. The broad four-domain audits retain default rank 100.

1. Corporate-event terms: US issuer tender offers, amendments and cancellations.
   Start with EDGAR SC TO-I / amendments and linked offer documents. Reconstruct
   rights before investigating outcomes. SEC documents its historical submission
   interface at https://www.sec.gov/search-filings/edgar-application-programming-interfaces.
2. Prediction resolution: versioned Polymarket questions, dispute and settlement
   records, and quote-history coverage. Current documentation cannot reconstruct
   a historical contract. Separate payout from real-world event probability.
3. Stablecoin redemption: USDC banking interruptions, issuer announcements and
   native-venue price coverage. Separate redemption eligibility and liquidity
   from reserve impairment; never assume government protection or a peg recovery.

Each audit has two hours including a 15-minute reporting reserve, zero model
trials and outcome access NONE. It must produce one registered successor or a
specific blocker, not open that successor's outcomes. Longer target horizons,
including months or years, are allowed within the same wall-clock test budget.

Secondary leads: collateral-driven forced flows, keeper participation, physical
storage/delivery constraints and policy-linked specialist businesses. They need
access and data feasibility before promotion. Quality/value and diversified trend
are relevant controls, not reasons to reopen rejected BTC or social candidates.
Treasury's ECIP archive can corroborate program facts behind manager claims:
https://home.treasury.gov/policy-issues/coronavirus/assistance-for-small-businesses/emergency-capital-investment-program/archives.

## What remains untested / what would change the answer

We have not established point-in-time price/quote coverage, independent event
counts, licensing, execution access, predictive value or net economics. Source
audits can change the order if these are absent. Subsequent experiments need
registered sample definitions, strong baselines, realistic costs and reject gates.
No new confirmation, prospective, paper or live authority is granted.

## Opened outcomes

The historical narratives in the case records are already known and cannot be
called blind. Their reported returns and event endings are not independent
validation. Future trials must explicitly inventory any such overlap; no sealed
